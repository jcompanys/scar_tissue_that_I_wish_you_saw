    \section{Objective}
    This report briefly summarizes the main findings from the notebooks \texttt{01\_scar\_exploration.ipynb} and \texttt{02\_clinical\_eda.ipynb}. The goal is to present the main visual results in a brief and clear way, while leaving space for final interpretation.

    A total of \textbf{168} cases were scanned in the dataset. Among them, \textbf{105} had a real core zone (CZ) segmentation available and were included in the scar exploration analysis. These 105 cases were split into \textbf{84 known cases} (80\%) and \textbf{21 held-out cases} (20\%).

    \subsection{Orientation used to define the AHA segmentation}
    The orientation of the polar maps was standardized before segment comparison. First, the left ventricular long axis was estimated from the anatomy. Then, the circumferential reference angle was defined from the basal ring orientation. This provides a consistent apex-to-base and angular alignment across patients.

    In summary:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item PCA on the LV shell estimates the long axis.
        \item The apex-to-base direction is aligned consistently.
        \item PCA on the basal ring defines the angular reference.
        \item The aligned LV is unwrapped into a polar map for cross-case comparison.
    \end{itemize}

\begin{figure}[H]

    \section{Clinical EDA}

    \subsection{Dataset overview}
    The clinical exploratory analysis begins with the assessment of variable completeness and general cohort structure. This is important because some descriptive summaries rely on fewer cases than the total cohort.

    The dataset contains NaNs in several variables, so some results should be interpreted with attention to the available sample size.

    The top missing variables are mainly follow-up and event-date fields: \texttt{date\_followup\_va} (100.0\%), \texttt{date\_icd\_therapy} (100.0\%), \texttt{date\_death} (99.4\%), \texttt{date\_ablation} (97.1\%), \texttt{date\_device\_implant} (94.2\%), and \texttt{date\_eef} (92.5\%). Other highly incomplete variables include \texttt{date\_clinical\_va} (88.4\%), \texttt{type\_followup\_va} (86.7\%), and \texttt{notes\_followup\_va} (86.1\%). This indicates that missingness is concentrated mostly in secondary event descriptors rather than in the core scar burden variables.

\noindent\textbf{Interpretation:} The dataset overview shows that the most substantial missingness is concentrated in follow-up and event-detail variables, while the main scar burden variables are more complete and therefore more reliable for the central descriptive analyses.

\subsection{Outlier handling}
No strict outlier removal was applied. The goal was to preserve all cases and keep the natural variability of the cohort. Since there was no strong domain-based criterion to decide which unusual values should be excluded, it was more transparent to retain them.

\noindent\textbf{Interpretation:} Preserving the full variability of the cohort was preferred over aggressive filtering. Since unusual values may still represent true clinical variability, a strict outlier exclusion strategy would require stronger clinical and domain-specific guidance.

\section{Closing Summary}
Overall, the project combines two complementary views of scar: a spatial view based on 3D alignment, 2D polar maps, and ISOMAP; and a clinical view based on descriptive EDA, risk factor prevalence, correlations, subgroup comparisons, and outcome-related scar summaries.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusion:} Overall, scars tend to accumulate more in the inner LV rings, especially in mid-apical and apical regions. Scar shape also seems to follow some recurrent patterns, including a possible XYZ-like morphology, although this still requires further investigation. Clinically, age does not appear to be a strong correlate of scar percentage in this cohort.
